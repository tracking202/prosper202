Release notes

Version 1.9.79
-- New: An AI assistant connected through MCP (p202 mcp, or Tracking202's hosted MCP server) renames and deletes traffic sources, their accounts and categories: ppc_network_update, ppc_account_update and aff_network_update run p202 ppc-network, ppc-account and aff-network update (a rename, an account's default flag, an account moved to another of your sources), and ppc_network_delete, ppc_account_delete and aff_network_delete their delete (dry_run first). Until now it could only list and create them, so asked to rename "Google Ads - Main" to "Main" it could only make a second source. A delete removes that one record, as Setup › Traffic Sources and Setup › Categories do: a traffic source's accounts and a category's campaigns stay, and each tool's description says so. A hosted connection needs no new approval for them: the access it was approved with already covers traffic sources and categories
-- Changed: A redirector re-click (?lpr=) keeps any c1 to c4 value it does not bring; it rewrote all four
-- Changed: A redirector re-click (?lpr=) keeps the traffic source and cost of the click's first visit, and whether that visit was filtered or a bot, and takes its campaign, landing page and payout from the visit it makes now; a click that has converted keeps its lead and value on the new visit. A re-click's row took the redirector link's traffic source and cost and no lead, and the click filter's verdict on the re-click itself, which marks a second click from one address within a day as filtered. On a re-click the Landing Page Optimizer's context token carries the click as stored (its traffic source account, its keyword and its c1 to c4), where it carried the link's traffic source account and this visit's keyword and c1 to c4. Two requests on one click in the same second are one visit: the later rewrites the earlier's rows instead of adding a row the reports would count again. A re-click that deadlocks with another request (two re-clicks of older clicks at once can, on an install whose click table is partitioned) is tried again, up to four tries in all with a short pause between, instead of ending on the database error page; under eight concurrent re-clickers, about 1 in 500 still lost all four
-- Changed: Every report counts a click a redirector link re-clicked once, under its latest visit: that visit's campaign, landing page, rotator route and time, with the traffic source and cost the click keeps from its first visit. They counted it at each visit, each under its own campaign, traffic source and cost, and counted its lead and income at each visit that held them, so a re-clicked click added clicks and cost, and once it converted leads and income, on every visit. A click from Monday re-clicked on Friday is now counted on Friday, at Monday's cost, and no longer on Monday. This holds for the Overview (its Rotator Breakdown too), Analyze, the group report, Spy and Visitors (and the Visitors download), GET /reports/* and GET /rotators/{id}/stats (p202 report, p202 rotator stats), the attribution breakdown and its exports, the LTV acquisition breakdown, customer and company engagement and recommendations, the daily email, the legacy GET /api/v1/reports/ and /api/v2/reports/, and the Android installs in GET /apps/report. GET /clicks still lists each visit. GET /system/retention's clicks_remaining for a deletion scheduled by click id counts each click once, where it counted each visit
-- Fixed: An attribution journey listed a click a redirector link re-clicked as one touch per visit; a journey built now holds it once, named by its latest visit, and its converting click is its last touch only. A journey built before keeps its touches until it is rebuilt. In the click cohort a click's credit and assists fall at the time of its latest visit, once
-- Changed: A conversion on a click a redirector link re-clicked is credited to the click's latest visit: recorded under that visit's campaign, at that visit's payout and under its campaign's payout mode, with that visit's time. It was recorded under whichever of the click's visits the database read first, usually the first, so a sale of the offer the re-click sent the visitor to was recorded on the first visit's campaign. This holds wherever a conversion is recorded (the pixels and postbacks, POST /conversions, Update Subids, revenue uploads, goals and web events, app installs), and GET /clicks/{id}/conversions (p202 click conversions) shows the latest visit's value. A click that converted and was re-clicked before 1.9.79, whose re-click row was stored as no lead, is still one conversion for a pixel or postback without a transaction id, and for Update Subids: they ask whether any visit is a lead. The per-campaign postback (pb.php, with or without event=) accepts a click any of whose visits was on the campaign it names, when that campaign is the click's account's, and credits the latest visit; it answered 404 "Unknown subid for this campaign" for any campaign but that of the visit the database read first
-- Changed: Update CPC (the page, POST /clicks/cpc, p202 click update-cpc) chooses a click a redirector link re-clicked by its first visit, whose time must be in the range and whose campaign, traffic source and other filters must match, and sets the cost of every visit of it. It chose each visit by its own time, so a range holding only a re-click repriced that visit of a click that arrived on another day, and a range holding the first visit left the later visits at the old cost. The check counts such a click once, updated counts the clicks whose cost changed (it counted rows, a re-clicked click once per changed visit), and the reports rebuild every hour a visit of the clicks is in, those after the range included. A click re-clicked before 1.9.79 that the cron job has not yet brought to one visit is taken at each of its visits in the range, and written from that visit on
-- Changed: A conversion's traffic-source pixels and postbacks (gpb.php, upx.php, and the server-to-server postbacks queued for them and for goals) use the traffic source account of the click's first visit, the source the visitor came from, and [[cpc]] is that visit's cost. They read whichever visit the database gave first, which could be a re-click's row with the redirector link's source and cost. A traffic source account of another account (possible on a click recorded by an earlier version) is now no source, and its pixels are not fired; they were
-- Fixed: A click's report row that could not be rolled up, on the tracking link after the click or after a conversion changed the click, was left as it was until its hour happened to be rolled up again for another reason. The click's latest hour is now queued for the cron job, which rolls it up on each run until it succeeds. This also covers the window between replacing the files with 1.9.79's and running its upgrade, in which every click's rollup fails: the cron job takes the queued hours once the upgrade has run
-- Changed: The upgrade to 1.9.79 does not walk your clicks: it notes the newest one, and the cron job then brings every click re-clicked through a redirector before the upgrade to one visit each (every visit takes the first visit's traffic source, cost and filtered and bot flags and, for a click that converted, its lead and value; the latest visit is recorded and the click rolled up again). Each minute's cron run spends about 10 seconds on it (at least one click), reading 5,000 click ids at a time, so a large install takes many runs. Until it reaches a click, the reports count that click at each of its visits, as 1.9.79 did, each under the traffic source and cost the visit was recorded with. A click it cannot bring over is written to the server's error log and the next run starts with it; a click whose visits belong to more than one account is left as it is and logged. It needs the cron job running
-- Fixed: Update › Reset Campaign Subids (POST /conversions/subids/reset, p202 conversion reset-subids) clears a click a redirector link re-clicked with the campaign of its latest visit, where every report counts it and its conversion: it was cleared with the campaign of any of its visits, so a reset of the campaign a click arrived on cleared a sale every report showed under another
-- Fixed: Web events from p202.track() on a landing page were lost (event.php answered 500) between replacing the files with 1.9.79's and running its upgrade; they are recorded, reading each click at every visit until the upgrade has run. An upgrade from 1.9.4 or earlier stopped at 1.9.5 on every run, its report rebuild reading a table a later step makes
-- Fixed: Security. My Account › 3rd Party API Integrations listed the first account's Direct Network Integrations, with their API keys, to every user who could open the page, and never the networks the signed-in user had added. It lists the signed-in user's own networks, and no network's API key reaches the browser: the list says a key is saved and, for a key of 12 characters or more, its last four characters; an edit leaves the key field empty to keep the saved key, or takes a new one to replace it (moving to another network needs that network's key); and the import progress is asked on the server, for the user's own networks. Its progress endpoint now asks for the page's permission. A network edit that is refused comes back to the edit, not to an empty Add a network form. If other users can open that page on your install, get new API keys from your networks and save them there
-- Changed: GET /system/dataengine (p202 system dataengine) says how far the cron job has brought the clicks re-clicked before 1.9.79 to one visit each (reclick_backfill: percent, clicks brought over, and what reports show meanwhile), and null once it is done. The walk asks its 10-second budget before each click rather than each 5,000 click ids, so a run no longer holds the cron's other jobs for a minute or more, and a click with very many visits (one re-clicked every minute for months) no longer stops it for good
-- Fixed: Security. GET /users/{id}/preferences (p202 user prefs get and user:prefs:get, and the answer the update commands print) served the account's integration secrets as stored: the ClickBank, JVZoo, Zaxaa and IPQS keys, the Slack webhook, the RevContent id and secret, and the Landing Page Optimizer's keys. It answers each one only as whether one is stored ({"stored": true} or false), and the CLIs print (stored) or (not stored); PUT /users/{id}/preferences still sets them from a string. A column is served only once it is listed as holding no credential, so a column a later version adds is never served by default. A body GET answered can be sent back to PUT whole: its other keys are accepted with the values GET gave them and refused with any other, and a secret's stored state sent back as given leaves the secret as it is (the body was refused for its keys the endpoint does not write). If an API key with access to your preferences was held by anyone who should not have these secrets, replace them at each network
-- New: Clicks record Google Ads' click ids for iOS traffic (gbraid and wbraid) and TikTok's click id (ttclid) beside the gclid, from direct links and from simple and advanced landing pages (the landing page script sends them on from the page's URL), so a Google offline-conversion upload or a TikTok integration can match those clicks. Each is stored as it came, cut to its column as the gclid is: 150 characters for gbraid and wbraid, and 1,000 for ttclid, whose real values run to about 340. The redirector records none of them, as it records no gclid or UTM values. GET /clicks and GET /clicks/{id} serve all four (the gclid too, which they did not serve before), null for a click that came with none, as do p202 click list and click get and the Visitors download; the new [[gbraid]], [[wbraid]] and [[ttclid]] tokens fill them into offer and landing page URLs and traffic source pixels and postbacks. Clicks recorded before 1.9.79 have none. A click recorded between replacing the files with 1.9.79's and running its upgrade is stored without them, and the server's error log names the ids it did not keep
-- Fixed: A gclid or UTM value in the Visitors download that held a tab or a line break, as the visitor sent it, moved every column after it; it is written on one line, in its own column
-- Fixed: Clicks record Microsoft Advertising's click id (msclkid) and Meta's (fbclid) beside the gclid, from direct links and from simple and advanced landing pages (the landing page script sends them on from the page's URL), so a Microsoft offline conversion upload can match a click by its msclkid and Meta's fbc can be built from its fbclid. No version since at least 1.9.55 kept either id: the tables 1.9.51 and 1.9.52 made for them were written by nothing, so [[msclkid]] and [[fbclid]] filled with nothing, in offer URLs, pixels and postbacks alike (this repository's history goes from 1.9.29, which had neither, straight to 1.9.55, so whether 1.9.51 to 1.9.54 kept them cannot be read). A click recorded before 1.9.79 has neither id, and it cannot be recovered. Each is stored as it came, cut to its column as the gclid is: 150 characters for msclkid (Microsoft documents a 32-character id) and 1,000 for fbclid (real ones run to about 200, past the 150 the old table held), and compared case for case, as Meta says its id must be. GET /clicks and GET /clicks/{id}, p202 click list and click get and the Visitors download carry both, null for none; [[msclkid]] and [[fbclid]] fill them into offer and landing page URLs, traffic source pixels and postbacks, and the Setup pages' placeholder buttons offer both. While the database is down, a direct link's cached redirect fills them from the link as it does the gclid, where it left [[msclkid]] and [[fbclid]] in the URL. The redirector records neither, as it records no gclid. A click recorded between replacing the files with 1.9.79's and running its upgrade is stored without them, and the server's error log names the ids it did not keep
-- Fixed: p202 report summary and p202 dashboard with --profiles, --group or --all-profiles added up every field for the TOTAL row, ratios included, so two profiles at ROI 200% and 0% on equal cost showed a TOTAL ROI of 200%. The TOTAL now adds the counts and the money and computes EPC, average CPC, conversion rate, ROI and CPA again from those sums, as GET /reports/summary answers for all the profiles' traffic at once and to the same decimals (0 where a denominator sums to 0). Money in different currencies is never added: GET /reports/summary now answers the account's currency (currency), and when the profiles' currencies differ, or a server before 1.9.79 does not state one, the TOTAL leaves out the amounts and the ratios that need them (EPC, average CPC, ROI, CPA), keeps the counts and the conversion rate, and says what it left out and why: meta.not_totalled and meta.warnings under --json, Warning: lines on stderr otherwise. A field the CLI does not know (a newer server's) stays in each profile's row and is left out of the TOTAL. A profile whose answer holds a count or amount that is not a number is reported as a failed profile, as an unreachable one is, instead of ending the command; a table now says on stderr which profiles failed, and the error when every profile fails names each
-- Changed: An account starts with the privacy setting (Personal Settings, GDPR & privacy; user_pref_privacy) at "Enabled for European Traffic" (eu), where it started at "Disabled": every account made from 1.9.79 on, on a new install and on an upgraded one, whichever way it is made (the installer, Settings › Users, POST /users, p202 user create). Its visitors GeoIP places in Europe get no tracking cookies and are stored with a masked address (IPv4 /24, IPv6 /48), and so does every visitor GeoIP cannot place: a private or reserved address (a test click from your own network included), and every visitor where no GeoIP database is installed. An account that existed before the upgrade keeps the setting it had, Disabled included, and nothing about its visitors changes. The setting in force is the stricter of the install's (the first account's) and the link owner's, so an account added to an upgraded install holds back its European visitors even while the first account is Disabled, and on a new install the first account's setting holds back every account's; set an account to Disabled on Personal Settings (or PUT /users/{id}/preferences, p202 user prefs update --user-pref-privacy disabled) to track its visitors in full
-- Fixed: Security. Renaming a traffic source on Setup › Traffic Sources did not check whose source it was, and the rename moved the source into the account of the user who saved it: a user who could open Setup could rename another user's traffic source and take it, with the accounts under it left behind in the owner's account. A traffic source is renamed now only when it is the signed-in user's own and not removed; any other is refused with "That traffic source was not found on this account. Nothing was saved." and nothing is written, and the Slack notice of a rename names the source's stored name, not one from the page's address. The other Setup saves (categories, campaigns, landing pages, text ads, traffic source accounts, redirectors and Get Links) and the legacy WordPress API already checked that the record was the user's; each also names the user in the statement that writes it now. After updating, check each user's Setup › Traffic Sources for a source that is missing
-- New: An install admin can find and move back the traffic sources the rename took before 1.9.79. GET /system/traffic-source-owners (p202 system traffic-source-owners show) lists every traffic source that another user's traffic source accounts name, with the user it is in now, each user whose accounts name it, and on their accounts the traffic source accounts, clicks and conversions. POST /system/traffic-source-owners/repair (p202 system traffic-source-owners repair <id>, or --all) moves a source to the user whose accounts name it, one transaction per source, queues those accounts' clicks for the report rollup, and logs the move; only the source's user changes, its name stays as the rename left it. Nothing is moved by itself, the upgrade included: the same rows also come from traffic source accounts the API put on another user's source before 1.9.77 and from moves made on purpose, so the report marks a source ambiguous when its own user has accounts on it too, when several users' accounts name it, when an account was saved after the source was (or in the same second), or when a user no longer exists or is deleted, and an ambiguous source moves only to a user the operator names (to_user_id, --to-user-id), never to a deleted one. Both need an Admin with access_to_settings; a repair also needs a user who may act on both users, so only user 1 moves a source into or out of user 1's account. A repair can send back the owner and repair_to_user_id the report showed (the CLI always does, and its --all repairs the sources it showed one at a time), and a source that changed since is refused and left as it is. A source taken with no traffic source account under it leaves no sign and is not listed
-- New: Account › Settings tells an admin about the traffic sources in another user's account, for owners who use neither the API nor the CLI. When GET /system/traffic-source-owners lists anything, an Admin or the Super user whose role has access_to_settings (the routes' own checks, asked of the signed-in user, who must still be switched on) sees a notice above the tiles saying how many sources are listed and how many need a user chosen. Its Review link opens a panel that lists each source as the report does: the user it is in, each user whose traffic source accounts name it with those accounts, clicks and conversions, and the user it goes back to or why you choose. Each row's Move back… opens a confirmation that names the source, the user it leaves and the user it goes to, or asks which of the users whose accounts name it; the move is POST /system/traffic-source-owners/repair's own code, sent the owner and target the panel showed, so it takes the same locks, offers no user the viewer may not act on, and refuses, in the panel the page lands on, a source that changed after the page was read. The form carries the session's token; the notice reads only whether anything is listed, and the clicks are counted when the panel is open
-- New: A traffic source that traffic source accounts of more than one user name, which no move back clears (wherever it is, another user's accounts still name it, so it was listed again and Account › Settings' notice never went away), can be split. POST /system/traffic-source-owners/split (p202 system traffic-source-owners split <id>, and Split… on the Settings panel for a source marked owner_has_accounts or several_users) leaves the source in the account it is in, untouched, and gives each other user whose accounts name it a new source in their own account with its name, its live custom variables and its removed mark; that user's traffic source accounts (removed ones too) move onto their copy, a report filter of theirs set to the source is set to the copy, and those accounts' clicks are queued for the report rollup, so the clicks already recorded on them are reported under the copy after the cron job's next run. The custom variable values recorded on those clicks before the split stay recorded against the source's own variables. The split is one transaction that locks the users, the accounts that move and then the source, reads everything again and writes nothing unless it still resolves the same way, so a failure part way leaves nothing written; it is logged. It takes back the owner and the users the report showed (owner_user_id, copy_to_user_ids; the CLI and the panel always send them) and refuses a source that changed since, copies nothing for a deleted user or one with no record, and needs an Admin with access_to_settings who may act on the user the source is in and on each user who gets a copy. To have the source end up with another user, move it there first, then split it. GET /system/traffic-source-owners also says how many custom variables each source has (custom_variables)

-- Fixed: On a new install running MariaDB, clicks that arrived at the same moment could go unrecorded (the visitor was still redirected, or a landing page showed an error). They are all recorded now: the report rollup of a single click reads that click through its index instead of scanning the click table

Pages still help run the cron's work, but no longer count as a cron job. The "Cron job last ran" tile, the cron health check, GET /system/info, GET /system/cron and p202 system cron now show when a cron job or AutoCron last ran
-- Fixed: When the AutoCron service refuses an install's address (localhost, a private address or an IP address), Settings said the service "could not be reached". It now says AutoCron works only for installs at a public web address, and to use a cron job instead. Saving On while AutoCron is already on registers the install again
-- Fixed: After saving a setting on the My Account pages, the message appeared at the top of the page while the page scrolled down to the setting, so it was out of sight. It now appears in the section you saved

-- Fixed: An install whose 202-config.php predates the read-only database connection (it defines only getConnection()) no longer stops with a PHP error on the pages, Spy and the API; the read connection falls back to the main one
-- Fixed: An advanced landing page's script stays valid when a visitor's device name or the page's ids hold text that is not UTF-8; such text broke the page's tracking script for that visit
-- Fixed: A ClickServer key check reads the licence service's answer by its HTTP status, and an answer it does not recognise counts as no answer: the page says the key could not be checked and saves nothing
-- Fixed: p202 conversion simulate takes the click id from the field the campaign's offer URL puts [[subid]] in (Awin's clickref, HitPath's c1, Partnerize's /pubref:), matching each rotated offer URL exactly. It refuses rather than guess when rotated URLs would give different ids or no campaign URL decides; --subid-field names the field then. The SubID parameters page lists the networks' published fields
-- Fixed: A new install stopped on step 2, "Get your license key", with a server error, so the key had to be entered by hand. The page now opens on a new install, before Prosper202 has created its database tables. On an install that is already set up, a license key sent back to that page is no longer repeated in the address of the sign-in page it moves you on to

GET /api/v3/connect, POST /api/v3/connect/token and POST /api/v3/connect/revoke serve it, and GET /users/{id}/api-keys answers each key's label

-- Fixed: bulk-upsert answered rows created or updated that a deadlock had rolled back. Its rows are written up to 100 at a time in one transaction; a deadlock undoes the whole of it, and the rows after it were then written outside any transaction. A batch a deadlock rolls back is now written again from the start, up to three more times, and if it still cannot be, every row of it is answered error with retryable: true, counted in summary.retryable: send those rows again under a new Idempotency-Key. A lock wait timeout is answered that way at once, and the rows after it are not attempted. A database failure after a batch was stored answered 500 and left the Idempotency-Key unspent, so the same rows sent again stored that batch twice; every row is now answered and the answer kept under the key, and a batch whose commit the database did not confirm is answered unconfirmed: true, never retryable. A row that failed after its insert (taking its public id, or being read back) was answered error and kept anyway; it is now undone, and its neighbours are stored. The change feed (GET /changes/{entity}) hears only of rows that were stored, once each
-- Fixed: A failed read of the stored database version, for any reason but a missing version table, was read as version 1.0.2: every signed-in page sent the operator to the upgrade, and an upgrade run then would have started again from its first step. Only a missing table reads as 1.0.2 now; any other failure shows the database error page
-- Fixed: Setup › Redirector's rule save went on past a database failure: a failed update of the redirector skipped every rule and ended in a PHP error, and a rule, redirect or criterion the database refused was dropped with the rest reported saved. Each failure now shows the database error page and is recorded. A campaign or landing page named with an apostrophe ("Mike's offer") made its redirect fail to save; it is stored as named. With Slack on, saving an existing rule sent its name and status notices with no old value
-- Fixed: Get Links, removing a link and Setup › Campaigns, Categories, Text Ads and Landing Pages act on a failed database read: a link whose details could not be read was deleted anyway, a link was saved whose traffic source variables could then not be read (they are read before anything is saved now), and the Landing Page Optimizer link read a failed lookup as "not paired". After a link's edit is saved, a Slack notice whose names cannot be read is skipped and the page says so above the link
-- Fixed: The pixels and postbacks (gpx.php, upx.php, gpb.php, cb202.php) record a database failure with the database errors and answer 500: gpx.php dropped the conversion with nothing recorded, and upx.php answered "Click data not found" for a failed read, or recorded a conversion and fired none of its traffic source's pixels. A direct link's CPA row the database refuses is recorded the same way, after the click's report row, which no longer waits on it
-- Fixed: On a tracking link, a database session that refused its sql_mode was used anyway, and the click written under the server's own mode. It is now logged and read as an outage: the visitor goes to the URL the link keeps for one, or gets the 503 page, and nothing is written. -- New: Goals. A goal is a named outcome — an install, an event (with conditions on its properties if you like), the Nth occurrence, a running sum, a step after another goal, within a window — and what reaching it is worth. A goal belongs to a campaign, an app registration or the account, and web campaigns and both app platforms share the same goals. Every edit is a new version and a conversion keeps the version that produced it; re-evaluation previews, then applies, a version to past clicks and installs. A campaign pays for a goal at its own payout, and an untrusted value (revenue reported by p202.track(), say) is recorded but never paid.
