# Click throughput, phase 1: one database connection per click, and lost clicks counted.
-- Changed: A click opens one MySQL connection, not two. 202-config.php opened a read connection beside the primary on every request, though only Analyze › Spy reads through it, so under load Apache's workers asked MySQL for twice its default max_connections and every refused connection was a click sent on without being saved. The config written from 1.9.80 opens the read connection the first time something reads through it (none at all when the replica setting names the primary's server, and the primary when the replica cannot be reached, logged); a 202-config.php written by an earlier release keeps working, and the click scripts close its read connection as soon as it is open. The signed-in pages no longer open it either, except Spy.
app: Changed: Each click uses one database connection instead of two, so a busy tracker loses no clicks to MySQL's connection limit.
-- New: Clicks redirected without being saved are counted. When the database refuses the connection (too many connections, or down), dl.php, lp.php and off.php still send the visitor to the URL they keep for an outage, and the click is lost; each one is now counted in 202-config/temp/unsaved-click-redirects.json, with the first and last times, and shown on Account › Settings, GET /api/v3/system/health (unsaved_click_redirects) and 202-cronjobs/health.php. A last one in the past 7 days adds a warning to each.
app: New: Account › Settings says how many clicks were redirected without being saved while the database was out of reach.
-- Changed: Docker: Apache's KeepAliveTimeout is 2 seconds (it was 5, which held every worker idle at 30-40 clicks per second) and MaxRequestWorkers is set at 150; the compose files' MySQL runs with max_connections 400 and the binary log off. innodb_flush_log_at_trx_commit=2 is documented as an opt-in (documentation/deploying-on-coolify.md).
